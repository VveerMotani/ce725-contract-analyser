How the tool works, and where data goes in

The tool reads a contract, pulls out six kinds of financial clause, flags risks, and shows what they do to cash. Data is needed at every step, and the Data Lead owns the first one.

STEP 1Collect and label contractsData Lead (you). Build the answer key.
STEP 2Extract clauses with the LLMAI and Tool Developer. Quote and section for every answer.
STEP 3Flag risksCompare each term with reference values.
STEP 4Estimate cash impactFinance Modeller. Retention, advance, delays, escalation.
STEP 5Score against the keyValidation Engineer. Correct, missed, incorrect.

A. Contracts to read

  • MoRTH standard documents: EPC and HAM forms
  • Revised standard EPC RFP, 2018
  • PPP model concession agreements
  • CPWD GCC 2023 (IIT Kanpur copy)
  • IIT Madras tender annexure, real numbers filled in
  • Live tenders on CPPP and GeM
  • World Bank standard documents, non-commercial use

Use: the text the tool reads. Two of these become the benchmark pair. FIDIC Red Book is paid, so it is not on the list.

B. Reference rules

  • GFR 2017 Rule 171: performance security, reported as 3 to 10% for goods
  • CPWD Form 8: 5% security deposit and 5% performance guarantee (older form)

Use: yardsticks for the risk flags, for example "retention above the norm". Confirm each number in the 2023 text before using it.

C. Price indices

  • eaindustry.nic.in: Wholesale Price Index by commodity, monthly
  • Labour Bureau indices for labour cost

Use: try "what if steel and cement rise X% during the contract". Base years differ and do not link, so pick one. A separate bitumen series was not confirmed.

D. Interest rates

  • RBI repo rate
  • Bank lending rates (MCLR) for overdraft cost

Use: turn a cash shortfall into an interest cost. Not looked up yet.

E. Legal context

  • Section 74, Indian Contract Act: damages must be reasonable
  • Supreme Court, 2015: loss can be presumed from delay, and the contractor must show no loss
  • Supreme Court, 28 April 2025: damages refused where the employer extended time and the contractor finished inside it

Use: the wording of LD warnings. The tool gives risk flags and is not legal advice.

F. CUAD practice set

  • CUAD on Hugging Face
  • 510 contracts, 41 clause categories, CC BY 4.0

Use: optional. Practise the extraction prompts and scoring on labelled data before touching Indian contracts. Not construction contracts.

2. Show what retention, advance and payment delay do to cash

Contractors fail on cash, not on profit. With the terms from the contract, the tool draws what the contractor has paid out against what has come in, and the biggest gap is the peak funding need. A 10 Cr, 10-month project is used here, and costs are 90% of the billed value.

Assumptions: advance paid in month 1 and recovered evenly from each bill, retention released in month 14, overdraft at 11% a year.

3. Size two risks: price rise and delay damages

If a contract is fixed price, a rise in cement and steel comes out of the contractor's margin. If it has liquidated damages, every late week costs a fixed share of the contract value. The tool reads the clause and shows the exposure next to the margin.

Margin here is 1.0 Cr (10% of 10 Cr). Materials are assumed to be 35% of contract value. LD is capped at 10%. For comparison, the NHAI EPC agreements charge 0.05% a day (0.35% a week) with a 10% cap, and CPWD GCC 2023 allows up to 1% a month with a 10% cap.

What the data cannot do

No real executed contractEverything is a standard form or a tender. Real contracts have extra negotiated terms, so the tool is tested on the easier case.
Some links are not opened yetThe NHAI agreements, the MP tender, CPWD GCC 2023, the 2022 procurement manual and both rulings were opened and read. The rest came from web searches. The register workbook marks each one.
Reference numbers need checkingPercentages for deposits, guarantees and delay damages came from summaries and older forms. Confirm each one in the current document.
Not legal adviceTwo Supreme Court rulings on delay damages were read, and they pull in different directions. The tool flags risks and does not say what a court would do.
No project or competitor dataThe data does not include a contractor's own costs or schedule. The cash toy needs a bill of quantities from a real tender.
Scanned PDFs may read badlyText extraction can drop tables and schedules, which is exactly where CPWD and MoRTH put the key numbers.
